the water

why does weight go up before a period?

by the maker of hæl · october 12, 2026

the short answer

it is mostly water. hormones late in the cycle make the body hold extra fluid, adding about a pound on average, sometimes more. it peaks around the first day of the period and is gone about a week later. it isn't fat, and it isn't a setback.

how much weight do you gain before your period?

less than it feels like, on average. a 2023 study that weighed women through a cycle found them about 0.45 kg (one pound) heavier during their period than at a point earlier in the cycle, and the whole difference was water held outside the cells. fat and muscle didn't change.1

an average hides a lot. some women see almost nothing on the scale; others see a few pounds that come and go on the same days every month. your own pattern says more than any study can, which is why it's worth watching across two or three cycles before reading anything into a single morning.

why does it happen?

the main explanation is water. estrogen and progesterone both affect how the kidneys hold on to salt, and where salt stays, water stays with it. as the hormones shift late in the cycle, many women carry extra fluid, which shows up as bloating, tender breasts, puffy fingers, and a higher number on the scale.4

appetite plays a part too. resting metabolism may run slightly higher in the luteal phase, the two weeks after ovulation, though the effect is small and newer studies find it smaller still.3 many women notice more hunger in those days, and a fuller gut also weighs something.

when does it peak, and when does it go?

later than most people think. a year-long study of 62 women and 765 cycles found that fluid retention climbed through the second half of the cycle and peaked on the first day of the period, not in the days before it. it was lowest in the middle of the follicular phase, roughly a week after the period ends.2

so the number often keeps rising for a day or two after bleeding starts, and settles over the following week. if you weigh yourself on day one of your period, you are likely weighing yourself at the wettest point of the month.

is it fat?

no. putting on a pound of fat takes days of eating well beyond what the body uses. a pound that appears over three days and leaves over five is water. in the 2023 study, body fat stayed flat across the cycle while the water came and went.1

what about birth control, or no period?

hormonal birth control that stops ovulation flattens the natural rise and fall of estrogen and progesterone, so the pattern may be smaller, different, or tied to the pill-free week instead. not every study finds a cycle effect even in women with natural cycles. if you don't have a cycle, or it isn't regular, comparing a morning with the same point last month is fairer than comparing it with yesterday.

how to read the scale across a cycle

  • weigh at the same time, ideally in the morning before eating, so the only thing changing is you.
  • compare a morning with the same day of your last cycle, not with yesterday. day 24 against day 24 tells you something; day 24 against day 10 mostly tells you about water.
  • look at the trend over weeks. a single morning is noise.
  • expect the highest number around the first day of your period and the lowest about a week after it ends.

swelling that comes on suddenly, swelling in only one leg, or swelling with breathlessness or chest pain is not cycle water. see a doctor, urgently for the last two.
