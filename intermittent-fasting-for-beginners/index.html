the short answer

intermittent fasting means eating within a window and not eating for the rest of the day. 16:8, sixteen hours without food and eight with it, is the common schedule. start at twelve hours and lengthen slowly. in trials it helps mostly by making it easier to eat a little less; it isn't a shortcut, and it isn't for everyone.

what is intermittent fasting?

intermittent fasting means eating within a window of the day and not eating for the rest of it. the food itself doesn't have to change. the common forms are named by their hours: 12:12 is twelve hours without food and twelve with it, 14:10 and 16:8 narrow the window, and 18:6 narrows it again. 5:2 is a different idea: two days a week of very little food, five of ordinary eating.

part of the interest is what happens as a fast goes on. once the sugar stored in the liver runs low, the body turns more to fat and makes ketones from it, a change researchers call the metabolic switch. it usually comes somewhere between twelve and thirty-six hours, depending on how much the liver had stored.1 that is one reason two fasts of the same length can feel so different.

what is 16:8?

sixteen hours without food and an eight-hour window to eat, every day or most days. a common version is to finish dinner by 8 pm and eat again at noon, so the night does most of the work. it is the schedule studied most, and the one most people mean when they say intermittent fasting.

does intermittent fasting work for weight loss?

it helps when it makes eating a little less easier; the timing alone does little. in a twelve-week trial of 116 adults, those eating within eight hours lost about 0.9 kg and those eating three meals across the day about 0.7 kg, a difference too small to count.2 in a year-long trial, two groups ate the same calories, one of them within an eight-hour window. the window group lost 8.0 kg and the other 6.3 kg, and again the difference wasn't significant.3

shorter windows change more, mostly by changing how much people eat. adults who ate within four or six hours for eight weeks ate about 550 fewer calories a day without counting, and lost about 3% of their weight.4 most of these trials were in adults with obesity, so results may differ for others. the honest summary: a window is a structure, and a structure can make an ordinary goal easier to keep. it isn't a metabolic shortcut.

how to start intermittent fasting

  1. start with twelve hours, most of them overnight: dinner by 8 pm, breakfast at 8 am. for many people this is already close to how they eat.
  2. lengthen slowly. add an hour every few days, toward fourteen, then sixteen if it still feels fine. there is nothing to win by getting there fast.
  3. keep drinking. water, black coffee and plain tea have almost no calories, so most people keep them during a fast.
  4. eat enough inside the window. fasting that turns into eating too little overall is where the real risks are, including to the menstrual cycle.
  5. keep rest days. a day without a fast is part of the practice, not a lapse from it.
  6. end a fast that feels wrong. dizziness, faintness or a racing heart are reasons to eat, not to push on.

what breaks a fast?

anything with real calories: food, milk in coffee, juice, a sweet drink. water, black coffee and plain tea don't. sugar-free gum and sweeteners are argued about; the amounts are tiny, and what matters more is whether they make the fast easier or harder to keep.

how long is too long?

daily fasts of twelve to sixteen hours are what most of the research covers. fasts of a day or more are a different practice with different risks, and anything past forty-eight hours should be supervised by a doctor.

who shouldn't fast?

some people should not fast without a doctor's advice:

  • during pregnancy or breastfeeding, or while trying to conceive
  • with a current or past eating disorder
  • with diabetes, or on medicines that lower blood sugar
  • under 18
  • with a missed or irregular period that hasn't been looked into
